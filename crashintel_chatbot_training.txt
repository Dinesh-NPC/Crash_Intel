Purpose:
CrashIntel processes road accident data using Apache PySpark,
visualizes crash patterns through interactive dashboards, and
predicts accident severity using a trained Logistic Regression
machine learning model. The platform supports smart road safety
decision-making by helping users understand when, where, and
under what conditions accidents are most likely to occur and
be severe.

Pages in the application:
- Dashboard: 7 interactive charts with month/day filters
- Data Explorer: Browse, search, filter and export all accident records
- Predict: ML-based severity predictor using 8 input factors
- Metrics: Model accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score
- About: Team and project information

SECTION 2 — DATASET INFORMATION
================================================================

Dataset: traffic_accidents.csv
Total Records: 209,306 accident records
Source: Road accident database

Column Descriptions:
- crash_hour: Hour of the day when accident occurred (0 to 23)
- crash_day_of_week: Day of week (1=Monday to 7=Sunday)
- crash_month: Month number (1=January to 12=December)
- num_units: Number of vehicles involved in the accident
- most_severe_injury: Worst injury in the accident (target variable for ML)
- weather_condition: Weather at time of crash (Clear, Rain, Snow, Fog, etc.)
- lighting_condition: Light conditions (Daylight, Dark, Dawn, Dusk, etc.)
- traffic_control_device: Traffic control present (Signal, Stop Sign, None, etc.)
- roadway_surface_cond: Road surface condition (Dry, Wet, Ice, Snow/Slush, etc.)

All PySpark analysis is done using groupBy operations on this dataset,
mirroring the Google Colab notebook analysis pipeline.

SECTION 3 — KEY DATASET FINDINGS FROM PYSPARK ANALYSIS
================================================================


SEVERITY DISTRIBUTION:
PySpark operation: groupBy("most_severe_injury").count()
- NO INDICATION OF INJURY: ~142,000 records (68% of all accidents)
- NONINCAPACITATING INJURY: ~38,000 records (18%)
- REPORTED, NOT EVIDENT: ~18,000 records (9%)
- INCAPACITATING INJURY: ~8,000 records (4%)
- FATAL: ~3,100 records (1.5%)

Insight: Most accidents result in no physical injury. However,
the 1.5% fatal rate still represents over 3,000 deaths in the dataset.

PEAK ACCIDENT HOURS:
PySpark operation: groupBy("crash_hour").count().orderBy("crash_hour")
- Highest risk: 5 PM to 6 PM (evening rush hour)
- Second highest: 3 PM to 4 PM (afternoon traffic)
- Morning rush: 7 AM to 9 AM is also high risk
- Safest hours: 3 AM to 4 AM (minimal traffic volume)
- Night hours (10 PM to 4 AM) have fewer accidents but higher severity rate
- Recommendation: Avoid driving during 4 PM to 7 PM if possible

DAY OF WEEK ANALYSIS:
PySpark operation: groupBy("crash_day_of_week").count()
- Most accidents: Friday (highest total)
- Second: Thursday and Wednesday
- Least accidents: Sunday
- Weekend nights (Friday and Saturday late night) have higher severity
- Recommendation: Extra caution on Friday evenings

MONTHLY TRENDS:
PySpark operation: groupBy("crash_month").count()
- Highest accident months: October, November, December
- Summer months June through August are also elevated
- Lowest: February (fewer driving days in the month)
- Recommendation: Winter months require increased road safety measures

WEATHER CONDITIONS:
PySpark operation: groupBy("weather_condition").count()
- Clear weather has the MOST accidents by count (due to high traffic volume)
- Rain significantly increases accident severity
- Snow and ice have fewer accidents but much higher fatal rate
- Fog has the highest severity ratio per accident
- Recommendation: In rain or fog, reduce speed and increase following distance

ROAD SURFACE CONDITIONS:
PySpark operation: groupBy("roadway_surface_cond").count()
- Dry roads: Most accidents by count (high traffic volume)
- Wet roads: Second most, with higher severity rate
- Ice: Fewer total accidents but very high severity
- Snow or Slush: High severity, especially for fatal incidents
- Recommendation: Wet and icy roads require significantly reduced speed

SECTION 4 — ML MODEL INFORMATION
================================================================

Model Type: Logistic Regression
Library: scikit-learn
Training Environment: Google Colab
Model File: model.pkl (loaded by Flask on startup)

Training Pipeline (mirrors PySpark MLlib):
Step 1 - StringIndexer: Converts text columns to numeric indices
Step 2 - OneHotEncoder: Binary encodes categorical features
Step 3 - VectorAssembler: Combines all features into one vector
Step 4 - LogisticRegression: Trains classifier on assembled features

Features used for training (8 input factors):
1. crash_hour — hour of day (0 to 23)
2. crash_day_of_week — day number (1 to 7)
3. crash_month — month number (1 to 12)
4. num_units — number of vehicles involved
5. weather_condition — weather at time of crash
6. lighting_condition — lighting at crash location
7. traffic_control_device — type of traffic control
8. roadway_surface_cond — road surface condition

Target Variable: most_severe_injury
Classes: NO INDICATION OF INJURY, NONINCAPACITATING INJURY,
REPORTED NOT EVIDENT, INCAPACITATING INJURY, FATAL

Training split: 80% training / 20% testing
Training records: 167,444
Test records: 41,862
Max iterations: 1000
Random state: 42

================================================================
SECTION 5 — MODEL PERFORMANCE METRICS
================================================================

Overall Accuracy: 73.62%
Correct predictions on test set: 30,820 out of 41,862

Per-Class Performance:
- NO INDICATION OF INJURY: Precision 73.8%, Recall 99.8%, F1 84.8%
- NONINCAPACITATING INJURY: Precision 32.3%, Recall 0.8%, F1 1.6%

Note on model performance:
The model achieves 73.62% accuracy but this is largely because 68%
of the dataset is the "No Indication of Injury" class. This is called
class imbalance — the model learns to predict the majority class more
often. In a production road safety system, techniques like SMOTE
oversampling or class_weight balancing would be applied to improve
detection of rare but critical classes like FATAL accidents.

The 73.62% accuracy means the model is correct about 3 in 4 times
on the test set, which is a reasonable baseline for a multi-class
imbalanced dataset.

SECTION 6 — SEVERITY PREDICTION GUIDE
================================================================

How the Predict page works:
Users enter 8 road condition factors and the trained Logistic
Regression model predicts the most likely severity class.

Risk Factors and their impact:
- Time of day: Night hours (10 PM to 4 AM) carry highest risk (+3 points)
  Rush hours (4 PM to 7 PM) carry moderate risk (+1 point)
- Weather: Rain, Snow, Sleet, Fog each add significant risk (+2 points)
- Road surface: Ice and Snow/Slush add high risk (+2 points)
- Lighting: Dark roads with no controls are very dangerous (+2 points)
- Traffic control: No signals or controls increase risk (+1 point)
- Vehicles involved: 3 or more vehicles add high risk (+2 points)
  2 vehicles add moderate risk (+1 point)

Prediction output levels:
- LOW RISK (Green): Score 0 to 4 — No Indication of Injury likely
- MODERATE RISK (Yellow): Score 5 to 7 — Non-Incapacitating Injury likely
- HIGH RISK (Red): Score 8 to 10 — Fatal or Incapacitating Injury likely

SECTION 7 — ROAD SAFETY DECISION SUPPORT
================================================================

For Drivers:
Q: When is the safest time to drive?
A: Early morning hours (5 AM to 7 AM) and midday (10 AM to 2 PM)
   have the lowest accident rates based on the dataset analysis.

Q: What weather condition is most dangerous?
A: While clear weather has the most total accidents, fog and snow
   have the highest severity per accident. Avoid driving in fog
   and snow whenever possible.

Q: Which day is safest to drive?
A: Sunday has the lowest total accident count in the dataset.
   Friday evening is consistently the highest risk window.

Q: How does road surface affect safety?
A: Icy roads increase fatal accident probability significantly.
   Even wet roads double the severity risk compared to dry roads.
   Reduce speed by at least 30% on wet roads and 50% on icy roads.

Q: What is the most common cause of accidents in the dataset?
A: The combination of high traffic volume (rush hour), dry roads,
   and clear weather accounts for the highest number of accidents.
   This is because more vehicles on the road means more opportunities
   for collisions, even in good conditions.

For Road Safety Authorities:
Q: When should traffic police be deployed for maximum impact?
A: Friday evenings between 4 PM and 8 PM account for the highest
   accident rates. Deploying enforcement during this window addresses
   the highest-risk period in the dataset.

Q: Which infrastructure improvement would reduce fatalities most?
A: Adding traffic signals at uncontrolled intersections and improving
   road lighting on dark stretches would address the top contributing
   factors to high-severity accidents in the dataset.

Q: What months need the most road safety attention?
A: October through December show the highest accident counts.
   Increased patrolling, road maintenance, and public awareness
   campaigns during these months would have the highest impact.

Q: How can we reduce severity even if we cannot reduce accident count?
A: The dataset shows that better lighting, proper traffic controls,
   and road surface maintenance (removing ice and slush quickly)
   significantly reduce severity even when accident volume stays high.

SECTION 8 — FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
================================================================

Q: What is CrashIntel?
A: CrashIntel is a road accident analytics platform built using
   Apache PySpark, Flask, and Machine Learning. It analyzes
   209,306 accident records to uncover safety patterns and
   predict accident severity.

Q: How does the severity predictor work?
A: It uses a Logistic Regression model trained on 167,444 records
   in Google Colab. You enter 8 road condition factors and the
   model predicts whether the likely severity is Low, Moderate
   or High Risk.

Q: What is Apache PySpark used for in this project?
A: PySpark reads the traffic_accidents.csv dataset and runs
   distributed groupBy operations to compute accident counts by
   severity, hour, day, month, weather, and road surface.
   These results power all the dashboard charts.

Q: How accurate is the ML model?
A: The model achieves 73.62% accuracy on the test set (41,862 records).
   This means roughly 3 in 4 predictions are correct. The main
   limitation is class imbalance since 68% of accidents result
   in no injury.

Q: What is the most dangerous time to drive?
A: Friday evenings between 5 PM and 7 PM, especially in rain or
   on wet roads, represent the highest-risk combination in the dataset.

Q: How many fatal accidents are in the dataset?
A: Approximately 3,100 accidents are classified as FATAL, representing
   about 1.5% of the total 209,306 records.

Q: What does the Data Explorer page show?
A: The Data Explorer lets you browse all accident records with
   search, filter by severity class, sort any column, paginate
   through results, and export filtered data as a CSV file.

Q: Who built this project?
A: CrashIntel was built by Dinesh Kumar T (23BIT023), Indira S (23BIT033),
   Jasir Ahamed M (23BIT035), and Kaviya Sree RS (23BIT042) from
   the Information Technology department at Kumaraguru College of
   Technology as a Semester 6 Big Data Analytics project.

Q: What is the difference between the Predict page and the Metrics page?
A: The Predict page uses the trained model to predict severity for
   a specific set of road conditions you enter. The Metrics page
   shows how well that model performed during training — accuracy,
   precision, recall and F1-score per severity class.

Q: Is this platform only for students?
A: While built as an academic project, the platform demonstrates
   real-world decision support capabilities. The same approach
   can be applied by road safety authorities, traffic departments,
   and urban planners to make data-driven decisions about road
   infrastructure and enforcement priorities.
