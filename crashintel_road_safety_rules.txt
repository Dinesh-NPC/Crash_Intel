SECTION 1 — GENERAL DRIVING RULES
================================================================

SPEED LIMITS (India — Motor Vehicles Act):
- Residential areas and school zones: 25 km/h maximum
- Urban roads and city limits: 50 km/h maximum
- State highways: 100 km/h for cars
- National highways: 120 km/h for cars
- Near schools, hospitals, markets: 25 km/h
- Expressways: 120 km/h for cars, 100 km/h for SUVs
- Heavy vehicles (trucks, buses): 80 km/h on highways
- Two-wheelers: 60 km/h on highways

Speeding facts:
- A pedestrian hit at 30 km/h has 90% survival chance
- A pedestrian hit at 50 km/h has only 50% survival chance
- A pedestrian hit at 80 km/h has less than 10% survival chance
- Every 1% increase in average speed produces a 4% increase in fatal crashes
- Speeding is a factor in 30% of all road fatalities in India

SEAT BELT RULES:
- Mandatory for driver and all passengers in India
- Fine of Rs. 1,000 for not wearing seat belt
- Seat belts reduce fatal injury risk by 45% for front seat occupants
- Seat belts reduce fatal injury risk by 60% for rear seat occupants
- Never tuck the shoulder belt behind your back
- Pregnant women must wear seat belts — below the belly, not across it
- Children under 12 must sit in the rear seat with seat belts

HELMET RULES (Two-Wheeler):
- Mandatory for rider and pillion passenger
- Helmet must be ISI marked (Bureau of Indian Standards)
- Fine of Rs. 1,000 for not wearing helmet
- Helmet must have chin strap fastened
- Helmets reduce risk of fatal head injury by 42%
- Helmets reduce risk of head injury by 69%
- Replace helmet every 5 years or after any impact

MOBILE PHONE RULES:
- Strictly prohibited to use handheld mobile while driving
- Fine of Rs. 5,000 for using mobile while driving
- Even hands-free calls increase reaction time by 40%
- Texting while driving increases crash risk by 23 times
- Looking at a phone for 5 seconds at 60 km/h means traveling
  83 meters completely blind
- Voice-activated systems are safer but still cause distraction

DRUNK DRIVING RULES:
- Legal Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) limit: 0.03% in India
  (30 mg of alcohol per 100 ml of blood)
- First offense fine: Rs. 10,000 and/or 6 months imprisonment
- Second offense: Rs. 15,000 and/or 2 years imprisonment
- Alcohol impairs judgment, coordination, and reaction time
- Even one drink increases accident risk by 1.5 times
- At 0.08% BAC the crash risk is 4 times higher
- Alcohol is involved in approximately 70% of night-time fatal crashes

DROWSY DRIVING:
- Being awake for 18 hours is equivalent to BAC of 0.05%
- Being awake for 24 hours equals BAC of 0.10%
- Drowsy driving causes 20% of all road accidents
- Most drowsy driving accidents occur between 2 AM and 6 AM
- Warning signs: yawning frequently, drifting lanes, missing exits
- If drowsy — pull over safely and rest for 20 minutes
- Avoid driving between midnight and 6 AM when possible

SECTION 2 — WEATHER AND ROAD CONDITION RULES
================================================================

DRIVING IN RAIN:
- Reduce speed by at least 30% in rain
- Turn on headlights even in daytime rain
- Increase following distance from 3 seconds to 6 seconds
- Avoid sudden braking — apply brakes gently
- Watch for hydroplaning — when tyres lose contact with road
  due to water layer (typically at speeds above 80 km/h)
- If hydroplaning occurs: ease off accelerator, do not brake hard
- Keep windshield wipers in good condition
- Check tyre tread depth — minimum 1.6 mm is legal minimum
- Heavy rain reduces visibility to less than 100 meters — slow down
- Never drive through flooded roads — 15 cm of water can stall vehicles
  30 cm of water can carry away a small car


DRIVING IN FOG:
- Use low-beam headlights — never high beam in fog (reflects back)
- Use rear fog lights if available
- Reduce speed significantly — visibility can drop to under 10 meters
- Do not overtake in fog
- Use road markings and edge lines as guides
- If visibility below 50 meters, pull over safely and wait
- Leave maximum following distance
- Fog accidents have among the highest fatality rates of any weather

DRIVING ON WET ROADS:
- Wet roads increase stopping distance by up to 2 times
- At 60 km/h on wet roads stopping distance is about 73 meters
  compared to 40 meters on dry roads
- Reduce speed by 20 to 30% on wet roads
- Avoid sudden steering inputs
- Check tyre pressure regularly — under-inflated tyres worsen
  wet road performance

DRIVING IN COLD / ICY CONDITIONS:
- Ice is most dangerous between 0°C and -5°C (black ice forms)
- Reduce speed by 50% or more on icy roads
- Stopping distance on ice can be 10 times longer than dry roads
- Accelerate and brake very gently
- If skidding — steer in direction of skid, do not brake hard
- Keep windshield clear of ice before driving
- Use winter tyres if available in cold regions

DRIVING AT NIGHT:
- Night driving accounts for 40% of all fatal accidents despite
  less traffic than daytime
- Use headlights from sunset to sunrise mandatory in India
- Dim high beams when within 200 meters of oncoming vehicle
- Reduce speed — your headlights cover only about 60 meters
  at low beam
- Be extra alert for pedestrians, cyclists, and animals on road
- Fatigue is more common at night — take regular breaks
- Dark roads with no street lighting significantly increase
  accident severity as shown in the CrashIntel dataset

SECTION 3 — TRAFFIC RULES AND SIGNALS
================================================================

TRAFFIC SIGNALS:
- Red light: Complete stop behind stop line
- Yellow/Amber: Prepare to stop — do not accelerate through
- Green: Proceed with caution — check for crossing traffic
- Flashing red: Treat as stop sign
- Flashing yellow: Proceed with caution, yield if needed
- Running a red light fine in India: Rs. 1,000 to Rs. 5,000
- Red light violations cause 20% of all urban intersection accidents

ROAD SIGNS — MANDATORY SIGNS (Red circle):
- Speed limit signs: Must not exceed shown speed
- No entry: No vehicles allowed
- No overtaking: Do not pass other vehicles
- No parking: Cannot stop vehicle here
- One way: Traffic moves in one direction only
- Stop: Complete stop mandatory

ROAD SIGNS — WARNING SIGNS (Yellow triangle or diamond):
- Sharp curve ahead: Reduce speed significantly
- School ahead: Reduce to 25 km/h, watch for children
- Narrow road ahead: Prepare for reduced width
- Slippery road: Reduce speed, avoid sudden maneuvers
- Pedestrian crossing ahead: Be prepared to stop
- Animal crossing: Watch for livestock or wild animals

LANE DISCIPLINE:
- Keep left except when overtaking
- Never straddle lane markings
- Use turn signals before changing lanes
- Check mirrors and blind spots before lane change
- Do not weave between lanes
- Buses and heavy vehicles must use left lane always
- Emergency vehicles must always have clear path — pull left

OVERTAKING RULES:
- Only overtake from the right side
- Never overtake on curves, hills, intersections, or pedestrian crossings
- Ensure road ahead is clear for sufficient distance
- Use indicator before and during overtaking
- Return to left lane after overtaking
- Never overtake a vehicle that has stopped at a crossing
- Sound horn before overtaking on rural roads

INTERSECTION RULES:
- Yield to vehicles already in the intersection
- At unmarked intersections — yield to vehicle on right
- Always check all directions before proceeding
- Reduce speed when approaching any intersection
- Intersections with no traffic control are the most dangerous
  as shown in CrashIntel data (traffic_control_device = None)

SECTION 4 — PEDESTRIAN AND CYCLIST SAFETY
================================================================

PEDESTRIAN RULES:
- Walk on footpath — if no footpath use right side of road
  facing oncoming traffic
- Cross only at designated pedestrian crossings
- Use zebra crossings and foot overbridges where available
- Make eye contact with drivers before crossing
- Do not assume a vehicle will stop
- At night wear reflective clothing or carry a torch
- Never cross between parked vehicles
- Look right, left, right again before crossing
- Pedestrians account for 15% of all road fatalities in India

CYCLIST RULES:
- Ride in the leftmost lane or dedicated cycle track
- Always use hand signals for turning
- Wear a helmet — reduces head injury risk by 85%
- Use front white light and rear red reflector at night
- Never ride on expressways or highways
- Maintain single file when riding in groups
- Do not use mobile phones while cycling
- Check brakes before every ride
- Be especially alert at intersections and driveways

PROTECTING VULNERABLE ROAD USERS:
- Motorcyclists, cyclists, and pedestrians are most at risk
- Give extra space when overtaking cyclists — minimum 1.5 meters
- Slow down near school zones, markets, and bus stops
- Watch for pedestrians stepping out from between parked vehicles
- In residential areas treat every road as a potential crossing point

SECTION 5 — VEHICLE SAFETY AND MAINTENANCE
================================================================

TYRE SAFETY:
- Check tyre pressure monthly and before long trips
- Under-inflation increases fuel consumption and blowout risk
- Over-inflation reduces grip and increases blowout risk
- Minimum legal tread depth: 1.6 mm (replace before this)
- Check for cuts, bulges, or embedded objects regularly
- Rotate tyres every 8,000 to 10,000 km
- Wheel alignment check every 10,000 km
- Bald tyres increase stopping distance by up to 50% in wet conditions

BRAKE SAFETY:
- Test brakes in a safe area if they feel different
- Squealing brakes indicate worn brake pads — replace immediately
- Pulling to one side when braking indicates brake problem
- Brake fluid should be changed every 2 years
- Never rest foot on brake pedal while driving (brake fade)
- ABS (Anti-lock Braking System) prevents wheel lock — let it work
  do not pump brakes with ABS, apply steady firm pressure

HEADLIGHT AND VISIBILITY:
- Check headlights, tail lights, indicators every month
- Replace bulbs immediately when they fail
- Clean headlight lenses — foggy lenses reduce light output by 80%
- Adjust headlights after suspension repairs or heavy loads
- Keep windshield clean inside and out — dirt reduces night vision

VEHICLE CHECKS BEFORE A JOURNEY:
- Check tyre pressure and condition
- Check all lights are working
- Check fuel level
- Check windshield washer fluid
- Adjust mirrors and seat position
- Check brakes feel firm
- Remove ice or condensation from all windows

SECTION 6 — EMERGENCY SITUATIONS
================================================================

IF YOU HAVE AN ACCIDENT:
1. Stay calm — switch on hazard lights immediately
2. Move vehicle to safe position if possible and not seriously damaged
3. Check for injuries — call 108 (ambulance) if anyone is hurt
4. Call 100 (police) for serious accidents
5. Do not move seriously injured persons unless there is fire risk
6. Set up warning triangle 50 meters behind vehicle
7. Exchange details with other driver — name, vehicle number, insurance
8. Take photographs of accident scene, damage, and positions
9. Get witness contact details if available
10. Report accident to insurance company within 24 hours

EMERGENCY NUMBERS IN INDIA:
- Police: 100
- Ambulance: 108
- Fire: 101
- National Emergency: 112
- Road accident helpline: 1073
- Highway patrol: 1033

VEHICLE FIRE:
- Pull over immediately and switch off engine
- Get all occupants out quickly
- Move at least 50 meters away from vehicle
- Call 101 (fire) and 112
- Never attempt to retrieve belongings
- Do not open bonnet if fire is suspected underneath

TYRE BLOWOUT AT SPEED:
- Do not brake suddenly
- Grip steering wheel firmly with both hands
- Gently ease off accelerator
- Steer straight — let vehicle slow naturally
- Once speed is reduced, gently brake and pull over
- Switch on hazard lights

VEHICLE IN WATER:
- Unbuckle seatbelt immediately
- Open or break window — do not open door until water enters vehicle
- Wait for pressure to equalize then open door
- Swim to surface
- Keep a window breaker tool in the vehicle

SECTION 7 — ROAD SAFETY STATISTICS — INDIA
================================================================

NATIONAL STATISTICS:
- India accounts for 11% of global road accident deaths
- Over 150,000 people die in road accidents annually in India
- More than 450,000 road accidents are reported every year
- India has one of the highest road fatality rates in the world
- Road accidents cost India approximately 3.14% of GDP annually
- Two-wheelers account for 44% of all road accident deaths
- Pedestrians account for 15% of fatalities
- Young adults aged 18 to 45 account for over 67% of deaths
- Majority of accidents (70%) occur on national and state highways

TOP CAUSES OF ROAD ACCIDENTS IN INDIA:
1. Overspeeding — 64.4% of accidents
2. Driving on wrong side — 4.7% of accidents
3. Jumping red lights — 1.7% of accidents
4. Use of mobile phone — growing contributor
5. Drunk driving — significant in night accidents
6. Poor road conditions — potholes, lack of markings
7. Lack of street lighting — major factor in night severity
8. Driver fatigue — especially long-distance truck drivers
9. Lack of helmet or seat belt use
10. Non-compliance with traffic rules

HIGH-RISK GROUPS:
- Young male drivers (18 to 25 years) — highest accident involvement
- Truck and bus drivers — long hours, fatigue risk
- Two-wheeler riders — most vulnerable on roads
- Pedestrians in urban areas — especially elderly and children
- Night-shift workers — fatigue and low visibility combination

GOVERNMENT INITIATIVES:
- Road Safety Policy 2011 — national framework for reducing deaths
- Vision Zero India — target to eliminate road fatalities
- Mandatory ABS for two-wheelers above 125cc from 2019
- Airbag mandate for all passenger vehicles
- Speed governors mandatory for commercial vehicles
- Reflective tape mandatory for heavy vehicles
- FASTAG electronic toll system to reduce congestion
- Motor Vehicles Amendment Act 2019 — significantly increased fines
- iRAD (Integrated Road Accident Database) — national accident tracking

SECTION 8 — SAFE DRIVING HABITS AND TIPS
================================================================

DEFENSIVE DRIVING PRINCIPLES:
1. Expect the unexpected — assume other drivers may make mistakes
2. Maintain safe following distance — 3 second rule minimum
   (double in rain, triple at night)
3. Scan the road ahead — look 12 seconds ahead at highway speeds
4. Check mirrors every 5 to 8 seconds
5. Be aware of all vehicles around you at all times
6. Have an escape route in mind at all times
7. Communicate clearly — use indicators, horn appropriately
8. Never drive when angry, upset, or distracted
9. Adjust driving for conditions — speed limits are for ideal conditions
10. Yield rather than insist on right of way — safety over pride

THE 3 SECOND RULE:
- Pick a fixed point ahead (sign, tree, overpass)
- When vehicle in front passes it, count "one thousand one,
  one thousand two, one thousand three"
- Your vehicle should not reach that point before you finish counting
- If it does, increase your following distance
- In rain: use 6 second rule
- At night: use 6 second rule
- Carrying heavy load: use 6 second rule
- Towing: use 8 second rule

LONG DISTANCE DRIVING TIPS:
- Rest for 15 minutes every 2 hours
- Do not drive for more than 8 to 10 hours per day
- Avoid driving between midnight and 6 AM
- Stay hydrated — dehydration increases fatigue
- Avoid heavy meals before driving — causes drowsiness
- Share driving with another qualified driver when possible
- Plan rest stops before starting journey
- Inform someone of your route and expected arrival time

CHILDREN IN VEHICLES:
- Children under 4 years must use approved child safety seat
- Children 4 to 8 years must use booster seat
- Children under 12 must sit in rear seat
- Never place rear-facing child seat in front passenger seat
  with active airbag
- Never leave children alone in vehicle — temperatures can rise
  dangerously fast

ROAD RAGE PREVENTION:
- Do not react to aggressive drivers
- Avoid eye contact with aggressive drivers
- Do not make gestures
- Maintain distance from erratic drivers
- If followed by aggressive driver — drive to police station
  or public place, do not go home
- Allow extra time for journey — being in a hurry causes aggression
- Remember every driver makes mistakes — be patient

SECTION 9 — ROAD TYPES AND SPECIFIC HAZARDS
================================================================

NATIONAL HIGHWAYS:
- High speed environment — maximum alertness required
- Maintain lane discipline strictly
- Use service roads for local access where provided
- Be alert for slow vehicles like tractors, bullock carts
- Watch for animals and pedestrians crossing at night
- Median crossings are extremely dangerous — never attempt illegal U-turn
- Rest stops every 2 hours on long highway journeys

URBAN ROADS:
- Constant hazard scanning needed — pedestrians, cyclists, auto-rickshaws
- Speed breakers and potholes are common — watch road surface
- Watch for vehicles emerging from lanes and driveways
- Be prepared for sudden stops by buses at bus stops
- Left turn on red is allowed in India only where sign permits
- Night driving in unlit urban areas requires extra caution

RURAL ROADS:
- Narrow roads often have no shoulders
- Animals on road are common hazard especially at night
- Road surface quality is variable
- Oncoming vehicles may not dim headlights — be prepared
- Overloaded trucks are common — give extra space
- Unmarked speed breakers are common
- Limited medical facilities in case of accident — drive with extra care

MOUNTAIN AND HILLY ROADS:
- Use low gear going downhill — do not ride brakes continuously
- At blind corners use horn to warn oncoming traffic
- Vehicle going uphill has right of way
- Check brakes before descending long hills
- Watch for falling rocks and landslide areas
- Pull over safely to allow faster vehicles to pass
- Keep left at all times on narrow mountain roads

SECTION 10 — ROAD SAFETY FOR SPECIFIC VEHICLES
================================================================

TWO-WHEELER SAFETY:
- Always wear ISI certified helmet with chin strap fastened
- Wear full protective gear — jacket, gloves, boots
- Do not carry more than one pillion rider
- Check tyre pressure daily — two-wheelers are sensitive to pressure
- Ride in the left portion of the lane
- Be visible — wear bright or reflective clothing
- Motorcycles are in the blind spot of large vehicles — avoid riding there
- Never filter between moving vehicles at speed
- Wet roads are extremely dangerous for two-wheelers — slow down significantly

BUS AND TRUCK SAFETY (for other drivers):
- Large vehicles have significant blind spots — four zones
  directly in front, directly behind, and on both sides
  If you cannot see the truck mirrors, the driver cannot see you
- Never cut in front of a truck or bus — their stopping distance
  is much longer than a car
- Give trucks extra space when they are turning — they need wide radius
- Watch for trucks drifting left before a right turn
- Trucks can blow out tyres — maintain safe following distance
- Never overtake a bus that is stopped at a bus stop — pedestrians
  may cross from in front of the bus

AUTO-RICKSHAW AND TAXI SAFETY:
- They can stop suddenly without warning at any point
- Maintain extra following distance behind autos
- They frequently change lanes without indicating
- Watch for passengers getting in and out — they open doors into traffic
